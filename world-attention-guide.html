How World Attention Should Be Read

One-page goal, process, and audit guide | Daily Briefing v2

Purpose. The goal is to identify 3–10 major attention clusters from the day, show where the attention comes from, and display the perceived source-orientation mix as percentages. It does not decide what is true or morally important.

The process

  1. Retrieve a live sample. At refresh time, the system requests Google News RSS front-page feeds for six English-language editions: United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, India, and South Africa. It reads up to 12 items per edition and records the retrieval time.
  2. Extract headline evidence. For each RSS item it retains the headline, named outlet, edition, and cleaned headline tokens. It does not read the full article or independently verify its claims.
  3. Group related headlines. Headlines are clustered when they share sufficiently specific terms. Common words and weak editorial wrappers are removed so repeated events can be recognized across editions.
  4. Measure attention. Each cluster receives a score: 12 points per represented edition + 4 per distinct outlet + 3 per article + 8 when the topic overlaps the briefing’s current trend list, capped at 100.
  5. Select the day’s major clusters. The current code prints up to 3. The intended design expands dynamically to 3–10 qualifying clusters, ranked by spread and recurrence while discouraging near-duplicates.
  6. Describe origin and perceived orientation. Show editions/regions, outlets, ownership families, and L/C/R/U percentages. Orientation comes only from reviewed source profiles; unreviewed outlets remain UNKNOWN.
  7. Add a TOK inquiry frame. Keyword rules assign a provisional category, Area of Knowledge, concept, suggested object, and question. These are prompts for examination, not conclusions about the event.
  8. Publish in two layers. The briefing keeps a fixed summary of the strongest 3 signals plus one TOK Lens card, with each representative headline wrapped in full. A separate WORLD_ATTENTION_MASTER worksheet shows all 3–10 qualifying clusters, complete headlines and links, percentages, sources, timestamps, and audit fields.

How to read the card

ATTNA recurrence index—not a percentage of the world, credibility, truth, or importance.
Editions / ArticlesHow widely and often the cluster appeared; edition share = represented ÷ sampled.
Source Mix / FamiliesDistinct outlets and known ownership families; unprofiled sources are disclosed.
Orientation L/C/R/UPercent of distinct outlets in each reviewed orientation bucket; UNKNOWN stays visible.
Why / AOK / ObjectRule-generated classroom prompts that require human scrutiny.
ATTENTION ≠ TRUTH • ORIENTATION ≠ CLAIM ACCURACY
Profile coverage = (L+C+R) ÷ all outlets; L/C/R/U percentages use all distinct outlets as the denominator.
