A Portfolio of Learning, Design, and Systems

Computer Science Teacher | Technology Educator | Designer | Builder | Adviser

Education

I grew up in Worland, Wyoming, where curiosity had plenty of room to wander. In high school, I learned that good teachers don’t just deliver content—they help students see what’s possible. One mentor showed me that teaching would eventually be part of my path, even though it took more than a decade to discover what I wanted to teach.

Growing up around computers sparked a lasting interest in how systems work—hardware, software, and the people who use them. That curiosity led me to earn a Bachelor of Science in Information Technology and a Master of Arts in Education. Those degrees built both technical skill and flexibility.

Since then, learning has remained ongoing through certifications, workshops, classroom experience, and independent projects. Education, for me, is not a finish line—it is a process of staying curious and willing to revise what I think I know.

Teaching

My teaching path has taken me through traditional classrooms, alternative schools, adult education, and online learning. Each environment shaped how I understand students and how learning actually happens.

At East High, I learned structure and classroom rhythm. At Stillwater Academy, I learned that trust and patience come before content. Granite Peaks Adult Education reinforced that growth has no age limit, and online courses showed that clarity matters even more when walls disappear.

At Skyline High School, I teach computer science, technology, and design while advising yearbook and student programs. I help students turn ideas into working systems—sometimes with code, sometimes with design, and sometimes by learning how to recover when something breaks.

Projects

Projects are where learning becomes visible. Each one begins with a question: How could this work better? Some projects solve classroom problems, some explore creative ideas, and others exist simply to test how systems behave.

I approach projects as experiments rather than finished products. The value is not just in what works, but in what teaches—about structure, usability, constraints, and iteration.

Art

Art is how I explore structure without strict rules. Through design, visual composition, and layout, I test balance, rhythm, and attention in ways technical systems sometimes cannot.

One idea that continues to guide my work is “repetition with constant variation.” Art becomes a way of noticing—seeing patterns, texture, and intention more clearly. Each piece is less about a final product and more about paying attention.

Photography

Photography is a practice of slowing down and noticing details that often go unseen. Light, shadow, texture, and timing all matter, and each image captures a moment that will not happen again.

I use photography to document environments, movement, and quiet transitions. It reinforces observation, framing, and intentional choice.

Music

Music is another system—built from patterns, tension, and release. I explore it through guitar, rhythm, and modal theory as a way to understand structure and emotion working together.

Rather than chasing perfection, I treat music as exploration. Improvisation and variation reveal how constraints can actually increase creativity.

Hobbies & Interests

Outside of teaching and building, I spend time in activities that reset perspective. Riding motorcycles demands full attention and presence, while language, astronomy, and music provide ways to stay curious and grounded.

These interests are not distractions. They sharpen focus, reinforce pattern recognition, and feed directly back into how I teach, design, and build systems.
